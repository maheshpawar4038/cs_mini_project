Core Cyber Hygiene Principles

  • Never share OTPs, passwords or UPI PINs: Treat these like private house keys.
  • Verify unexpected payment and refund requests: A PIN is exclusively for sending funds.
  • Check the sender's identity independently: Spoofed SMS headers and display names are easily faked.
  • Avoid unknown links and attachments: Shortened URLs (.bit.ly, .tinyurl) hide real destinations.
  • Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Use authenticator apps instead of SMS OTPs where possible.
  • Report suspected cybercrime early: Call 1930 within the "Golden Hour" to freeze fund transfers.

Academic Project Dossier

TruthLens — Suspicious Message and Scam Analyzer

Second Year B.Tech Computer Engineering Mini Project

Course: Introduction to Cyber Security

1. Problem Statement

People frequently receive suspicious messages through SMS, email, social media and digital payment platforms. Many users struggle to recognise common scam indicators and may disclose sensitive information or lose money.

2. Proposed Solution

A browser-based application that checks pasted messages against predefined security rules, highlights potential warning signs, calculates an indicative risk score, and provides actionable safety recommendations.

3. Technology Used

HTML5, CSS3, and Vanilla JavaScript (100% Client-Side DOM architecture with zero external APIs, zero server storage, and localStorage history).

4. Syllabus Mapping

  • Unit II: Cyber Crimes and Cyber Law — Credential phishing, identity theft, unauthorized access, and IT Act 2000 provisions (Sec 66C/66D).
  • Unit III: Social Media Overview and Security — Impersonation, task-based employment scams on Telegram/WhatsApp, URL shorteners, and social engineering.
  • Unit IV: E-Commerce and Digital Payments — UPI transaction mechanisms, collect requests, refund lures, and payment security principles.

5. Expected Outcomes

Improved awareness of phishing, impersonation, social engineering and payment fraud, together with safer online decision-making.
